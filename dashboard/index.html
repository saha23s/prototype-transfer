Motivation

Bird-sound models are learned from clean recordings of single birds, but are needed on noisy field recordings.

In short

  • On identical held-out soundscape clips, training on soundscape labels raises AP from 0.710 to 0.860. This is the gap we want to close.
  • The pretrained model's test AUC is 0.941 on soundscapes and 0.988 on focal clips. Part of this difference may reflect that focal data is in-domain for it and that the negatives differ.
  • Prototype detection degrades unevenly, even within one species. This is a hypothesis for where a method could act, not evidence that fixing prototypes will raise accuracy.

Why bird recognisers struggle in the field

Passive acoustic monitoring records whole landscapes for weeks. Most labeled bird audio, in contrast, is focal: a person points a microphone at one bird. A model learned from focal recordings must then work on soundscapes with distance, wind, overlapping species and long silences. Labeling soundscapes needs experts, so the target domain is where labels are scarcest.

We study AudioProtoPNet, a bird-sound classifier whose decision is a weighted sum of similarities to learned sound patterns (prototypes, five per species). Because each prototype can be inspected, we can ask which parts of the model stop working in the field, not only how much accuracy is lost.

What the prototypes reveal

Descriptive analysis of the pretrained model on clips outside the test split (training and validation sites and recordists; 14 species with at least 10 soundscape positives, 70 prototypes), so counts differ from the test-split numbers above. It uses soundscape labels, and its sites are the cross-validation sites: a method designed from these findings would have an optimistic cross-validation score. Treat them as hypotheses.
