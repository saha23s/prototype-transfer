Motivation

Bird-sound models are learned from clean recordings of single birds, but are needed on noisy field recordings.

The headline number, in plain terms

For one species, the model gives every 5-second clip a score. Sort the clips from highest to lowest. Average precision (AP) measures how well the clips that really contain the species are gathered at the top. We show it out of 100: it is 100 when every such clip is ranked above every other clip. A random ordering scores about the share of clips with the species, here about 2.5. Higher is better.

In short

  • On identical held-out soundscape clips, training on soundscape labels raises AP from 71.0 to 86.0. This is the gap we want to close.
  • The pretrained model's test AUC (the chance that a clip with a bird is scored above a clip without it) is 94.1 on soundscapes and 98.8 on focal clips (out of 100). Part of this difference may reflect that focal data is in-domain for it and that the negatives differ.
  • Prototype detection degrades unevenly, even within one species: on test sites 26 of 60 prototypes are clearly worse at detecting their bird on soundscapes than on focal clips (42 of 60 when both are compared against clips of other birds). This is a hypothesis for where a method could act, not evidence that fixing prototypes will raise accuracy.

Why bird recognisers struggle in the field

Passive acoustic monitoring records whole landscapes for weeks. Most labeled bird audio, in contrast, is focal: a person points a microphone at one bird. A model learned from focal recordings must then work on soundscapes with distance, wind, overlapping species and long silences. Labeling soundscapes needs experts, so the target domain is where labels are scarcest.

We study AudioProtoPNet, a bird-sound classifier whose decision is a weighted sum of similarities to learned sound patterns (prototypes, five per species). Because each prototype can be inspected, we can ask which parts of the model stop working in the field, not only how much accuracy is lost.

Notation used on this page

FocalA recording aimed at one bird (here from Xeno-Canto). The source domain. Shown in orange.
SoundscapeA recording from a fixed field recorder (passive acoustic monitoring, “PAM”). The target domain. Shown in blue.
ClipA 5-second piece of audio: the unit the model scores.
Development / test sitesDevelopment: the 70 non-test sites, used to compare methods. Test: 30 sites kept for final numbers.
Fold, cross-validation (CV)The development sites are cut into 3 groups. Each fold scores one group; training uses the other two.
Scores and “points”AP, AUC, cmAP and activations are shown out of 100. A change in points is the difference of two such numbers: 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points (not 6.7% more correct clips).
APAverage precision: how near the top of the model's ranking the clips with a species are; 100 = all first (worked example).
Soundscape AP (primary species)The main metric: AP on held-out soundscape clips, averaged over primary species.
AUC, ROC-AUCHow often a clip with the species scores above a clip without it. 100 = always, 50 = chance.
Primary speciesSecondary species with at least 30 positive clips in the soundscape sites being scored.
GapAP of the model trained on all labeled training-site soundscapes minus AP of the pretrained model.
95% interval, [a, b]The range the number covers when recording sites (focal: recordists) are resampled 1,000 times. A change whose range includes zero cannot be told apart from no change; this is not proof of no effect.
Reliably better / worseThe 95% interval of the change lies entirely above / below zero.
Secondary speciesSpecies with at least 5 positive clips in both the focal and soundscape sets being scored.
Δ, ΔAUC (prototypes)A prototype's AUC on soundscape clips minus its AUC on focal clips. Negative means it detects its bird worse in the field. Not a comparison between methods.
Failing prototypeA prototype whose ΔAUC is below −10 points.
Matched negativesOnly soundscape clips containing another target bird count as negatives, as for focal clips. “sc” stands for soundscape and “sc matched” for this version.
K, PkEach species has K = 5 prototypes (learned sound patterns); P1 to P5 name them, e.g. “whcspa P3”.
ask(x), activationHow strongly prototype k matches its best location in clip x: from 0 (no match) to 1 (shown out of 100 in charts).
Share of class evidenceThe part of a species' score (before the offset) that comes from one prototype, averaged over the clips with the species.
SilentThe prototype's activation is exactly zero on a clip.
Peak frequencyThe frequency at which an active prototype matches, as a median over clips with the species.
False matchOne of a prototype's strongest clips that is not labeled with its species.
Base rate, “base”How often the same thing happens among all soundscape clips without the species: the comparison for a prototype's false matches.
Positive / negative clipA clip that does / does not contain the species in question.
Median, q10–q90, q25–q75The middle value; the ranges holding the central 80% and 50% of values.
r = …Correlation between two sets of numbers, from −1 to 1.
nA count: the number of clips or items behind a figure.
kHz, s, melKilohertz (pitch), seconds, and the mel frequency scale of the spectrogram, which gives low pitches more room.
