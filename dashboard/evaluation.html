Evaluation protocol

One fixed way to score every model. Changing any part means re-scoring all models.

Metrics and how to read them

All scores are shown out of 100. If you read only one thing: soundscape AP is the main metric; everything else supports or explains it.

Average precision (AP), the main metric

Take one bird and sort all clips by the model's score for it. Go down the list. Each time you reach a clip that has the bird, note what share of the clips so far have it. AP is the average of those shares. It is 100 when every clip with the bird comes first, and mistakes near the top cost the most. A random order scores about the share of clips that have the bird, so rarer birds start lower and AP should not be compared between sets of clips in which birds are not equally common.

Numbers attached to a result

TermMeaning
Change, in pointsMethod minus pretrained model on the same clips. +6.7 points means the score rose by that much on the 0–100 scale; it is not a percentage of clips or a relative improvement.
Share of the gap closed (γ)The method's change divided by the change from training on all soundscape labels (development: 6.7 / 15.0 = 45%). 0% closes nothing, 100% matches that reference, and a negative value is worse than the pretrained model.
95% intervalHow much the change moves when recording sites are resampled. If the whole range is above zero the method is reliably better; below zero, reliably worse. If it includes zero we cannot tell, which is not evidence of no effect.
Folds ↑ / ↓Development uses 3 groups of held-out sites. These count the groups where the method is reliably better / reliably worse.
± valueHow much the score varies between the 3 folds. It is a spread, not a confidence interval.
Bold, underline, *Bold and underline mark the best and second-best scores; they say nothing about reliability. * means a test interval excludes zero.

1 · Species

Secondary: at least 5 positives in both the focal and soundscape evaluation sets (12 species on test). Primary: also at least 30 soundscape positives (7 on test).

2 · Headline metric

Soundscape macro average precision over the primary species. Also reported: soundscape ROC-AUC, cmAP over the secondary species, and focal AUC / AP to check that focal performance is retained. Focal recordings are part of the model's pretraining data (Xeno-Canto), so focal scores are an in-domain reference. All scores on this site are shown out of 100, and changes in points (for example 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points).

3 · Prototype transfer

For each prototype, Δ = AUCsoundscape − AUCfocal of its maximum activation as a detector of its species. Fails if Δ < −10 points; improves if Δ > +2 points. 5 prototypes per species. Soundscape negatives are all other clips; as a sensitivity check they are also restricted to clips with another target bird, which matches the focal negatives.

4 · Uncertainty

Cluster bootstrap with 1,000 resamples shared across models, so differences are paired. Soundscape clips are resampled by site (30 test sites), focal clips by recordist (110). Intervals are 95% percentile intervals.

5 · Development

Method choices are made on the validation split or 3-fold site-grouped cross-validation. The test sites are kept for final numbers.

6 · Use of labels and audio

Soundscape labels are used to evaluate. Methods that adapt may use unlabeled audio of training sites, never audio of held-out sites. All models are scored on identical clips.

Success criteria for a method

All four must hold for a method to count as closing the gap.

1AP gap closedat least 50% of the gap to the model trained on labeled soundscapes is closed, and the 95% interval of the improvement over the pretrained model excludes 0

Reading cross-validation numbers

  • Each fold has about 23 held-out sites. Results are shown per fold; a “mean ± sd” describes the spread over 3 folds and is not a confidence interval.
  • “Reliably better” means the fold's paired 95% interval of the difference to the pretrained model lies above zero (and “reliably worse”, below).
  • The scored species differ between folds, so failing prototypes are reported as a share of the prototypes scored on each fold, and method changes are also reported on the same species every fold (those primary on all folds, and those eligible on all folds).
  • The protocol's intervals compare each model with the pretrained model. The intervals between two adapted models on the results page use the same site resampling but are exploratory additions. Neither includes variation between training seeds.
  • Many methods are compared on the same folds without correction for multiple comparisons.

Notation used on this page

FocalA recording aimed at one bird (here from Xeno-Canto). The source domain. Shown in orange.
SoundscapeA recording from a fixed field recorder (passive acoustic monitoring, “PAM”). The target domain. Shown in blue.
ClipA 5-second piece of audio: the unit the model scores.
Development / test sitesDevelopment: the 70 non-test sites, used to compare methods. Test: 30 sites kept for final numbers.
Fold, cross-validation (CV)The development sites are cut into 3 groups. Each fold scores one group; training uses the other two.
Positive / negative clipA clip that does / does not contain the species in question.
Scores and “points”AP, AUC, cmAP and activations are shown out of 100. A change in points is the difference of two such numbers: 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points (not 6.7% more correct clips).
APAverage precision: how near the top of the model's ranking the clips with a species are; 100 = all first (worked example).
Soundscape AP (primary species)The main metric: AP on held-out soundscape clips, averaged over primary species.
AUC, ROC-AUCHow often a clip with the species scores above a clip without it. 100 = always, 50 = chance.
Primary speciesSecondary species with at least 30 positive clips in the soundscape sites being scored.
GapAP of the model trained on all labeled training-site soundscapes minus AP of the pretrained model.
95% interval, [a, b]The range the number covers when recording sites (focal: recordists) are resampled 1,000 times. A change whose range includes zero cannot be told apart from no change; this is not proof of no effect.
Reliably better / worseThe 95% interval of the change lies entirely above / below zero.
cmAPClass-mean average precision: AP averaged over species; here over the secondary species.
Secondary speciesSpecies with at least 5 positive clips in both the focal and soundscape sets being scored.
ΔAP, “change”A method's AP minus the pretrained (source) model's on the same clips. Positive is better.
Δ, ΔAUC (prototypes)A prototype's AUC on soundscape clips minus its AUC on focal clips. Negative means it detects its bird worse in the field. Not a comparison between methods.
Failing prototypeA prototype whose ΔAUC is below −10 points.
Matched negativesOnly soundscape clips containing another target bird count as negatives, as for focal clips. “sc” stands for soundscape and “sc matched” for this version.
K, PkEach species has K = 5 prototypes (learned sound patterns); P1 to P5 name them, e.g. “whcspa P3”.
γ, share of the gap closedA method's gain divided by the gap. 0% = no better than the pretrained model, 100% = equal to the all-labels model, below 0% = worse, above 100% = better than it.
How γ is averaged hereMean gain over the folds divided by the mean gap over the folds. The label curve page computes γ in each fold and then averages, so it can differ slightly (59% there, 60% here, for 250 labels).
↑ / ↓ (folds)Number of folds on which the method is reliably better / worse.
± valueStandard deviation over the 3 folds: how much they differ. Not a confidence interval.
Bold, underlineBest and second-best printed value among the methods compared. Says nothing about reliability.
*The test interval of the change excludes zero.
nA count: the number of clips or items behind a figure.
