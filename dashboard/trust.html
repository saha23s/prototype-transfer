When can a prototype be trusted?

Two exploratory tests on the pretrained model, using development-site soundscapes only.

Can we tell, without labels, when to believe a prototype? Agreement among a bird's own prototypes did not work. Competition from other birds' prototypes passed our preset tests, but the signal is modest, works for only some birds, and has not yet been shown to improve accuracy.

Test 1: do a bird's own prototypes reveal their reliability by agreeing?

Idea: if the 5 prototypes of a bird made independent mistakes, how often they agree would reveal how well each tracks the bird, with no labels. Reliability r is the correlation between a prototype's activation and the bird being present.

It does not work. The label-free guess averages 0.71; the measured value is 0.44. The prototypes also agree when the bird's presence cannot explain it, so shared mistakes look like reliability. For ordering a bird's 5 prototypes, the guess is no better than the weights the model already has. This was a feasibility probe with no preset rule, on 8,400 development clips.

Test 2: do other birds' prototypes compete for the same sound?

For 45 prototypes (the 5 of each bird with at least 30 positive clips) we took the 100 soundscape clips each matches most strongly: 4,500 matches. A match is the one spot on the spectrogram (time and pitch) where the prototype responds most. A match is contested if, at that same spot, a prototype of another of the 21 birds responds at least as strongly. Contesting uses no labels.

We then used labels and the experts' call boxes only to check each match: false (1,559), the bird is not in the clip; correct (2,593), the spot falls on that bird's call; unclear (348, left out), the bird is in the clip but the spot is elsewhere.

These were chosen after seeing the pass, so they are exploratory and do not change it. The same-spot signal scores higher both times, but we have not established that the location matters, or that the margin beats the prototype's own score. The rival's strength at the same spot is, on its own, better than a coin flip. (Counting any stronger rival anywhere in the clip as a contest is useless: it flags 100% of false and 92% of correct matches, so the table compares rankings instead.)

What this does and does not show

  • Shows: on these data, false strong matches are more often challenged by another bird's prototype than correct ones, and in contested false matches the challenger's bird is more often really there (70% against 43%).
  • Does not show: that using competition raises average precision. No method has been built or scored.
  • Limited to the pretrained model, each prototype's 100 strongest matches, the 21 birds, and the development sites. Test sites were not used. A method designed from these results would not get an independent score on those sites.
  • Next, if pursued: a competition step has to beat the same step without location (rival anywhere in the clip) and a simple rescaling of scores, on held-out folds.

Notation used on this page

SoundscapeA recording from a fixed field recorder (passive acoustic monitoring, “PAM”). The target domain. Shown in blue.
ClipA 5-second piece of audio: the unit the model scores.
Site, recordistWhere a soundscape was recorded; who made a focal recording. Splits keep each whole.
Development / test sitesDevelopment: the 70 non-test sites, used to compare methods. Test: 30 sites kept for final numbers.
Fold, cross-validation (CV)The development sites are cut into 3 groups. Each fold scores one group; training uses the other two.
Positive / negative clipA clip that does / does not contain the species in question.
K, PkEach species has K = 5 prototypes (learned sound patterns); P1 to P5 name them, e.g. “whcspa P3”.
ask(x), activationHow strongly prototype k matches its best location in clip x: from 0 (no match) to 1 (shown out of 100 in charts).
Scores and “points”AP, AUC, cmAP and activations are shown out of 100. A change in points is the difference of two such numbers: 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points (not 6.7% more correct clips).
APAverage precision: how near the top of the model's ranking the clips with a species are; 100 = all first (worked example).
AUC, ROC-AUCHow often a clip with the species scores above a clip without it. 100 = always, 50 = chance.
95% interval, [a, b]The range the number covers when recording sites (focal: recordists) are resampled 1,000 times. A change whose range includes zero cannot be told apart from no change; this is not proof of no effect.
MatchThe one spot on the spectrogram (time and pitch) where a prototype responds most strongly in a clip.
ContestedAt a match, a prototype of another of the 21 species responds at least as strongly.
Margin, rivalThe rival is the strongest other-species prototype at the match; the margin is its activation minus the prototype's own.
False / correct / unclear matchSpecies absent from the clip / spot on that species' call / species in the clip but spot elsewhere.
AUC of a signal (here)How often the signal ranks a false match as more suspect than a correct one, comparing matches of the same prototype with similar own scores. Out of 100; 50 = coin flip.
C0 to C4The five preset rules of the competition test.
†Too few false or correct matches to count in the rule.
Reliability rCorrelation, from −1 to 1, between a prototype's activation and its species being present. Not shown out of 100.
Order of the 5Rank correlation (−1 to 1) with the true order of a species' prototypes; 1 = same order.
