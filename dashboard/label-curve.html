Label curve

How many labeled soundscape clips does full fine-tuning need to close the gap?

What “all” means on this page

  • “All” is the pretrained model fine-tuned on every labeled soundscape clip of that fold's training sites: 5,520–5,640 clips, empty ones included.
  • It does not mean all the data. The held-out sites it is scored on and the test sites are never included, and it uses no focal training data.
  • The other points use a random N of those same clips (100 to 2,000); 0 is the pretrained model, unchanged. “All” is the reference that counts as closing 100% of the gap. It is not a proven upper limit.
250smallest tested label budget closing at least half of the gap on average
59%of the gap closed with 250 labels (folds: 53%, 79%, 44%)
98%closed with 2,000 labels
15.0total gap in soundscape AP, in points: all labels minus pretrained

What this page shows

  • With 100 labeled clips the effect is small and not reliable (0 of 3 folds reliably better; mean gap closed 8%).
  • At 250 labels the average gap closed passes one half, but the improvement is reliable on only 2 of 3 folds. From 1,000 labels it is reliable on every fold.
  • The 250-label threshold holds when every fold scores the same species: 250 labels close 61% on the 5 primary on all folds and 64% on the 7 eligible on all folds. These budgets are for full fine-tuning; a more label-efficient method might need fewer, so this is not yet the bar a label-free method must beat.
  • The curve flattens: 2,000 labels average 85.9, all labels 86.0.
  • The cost: training on soundscapes lowers focal AUC. The focal-retention criterion is met on 1/3 folds at 100, 0/3 folds at 250, 0/3 folds at 500, 1/3 folds at 1,000, 1/3 folds at 2,000 labels. The absolute drop is small, and these focal clips were part of the pretraining data, which favours the pretrained model.

What the labeled clips contain

Range over folds; species columns count positive clips (folds on which the species is primary). Clips are drawn uniformly at random, so 51%–60% contain no target species, and at 100 clips some folds have no positive at all for American Robin, Clark's Nutcracker, Hermit Thrush, Mountain Chickadee. Smaller sets are contained in larger ones.

How to read this curve

  • Each budget is one random draw with one training seed. The 3 folds give only a rough idea of the variation.
  • Labeled clips are spread over most training sites. Annotating a few sites thoroughly could give a different curve.
  • All budgets use the same number of optimiser steps, so small sets are repeated many times. The last epoch is used; no labeled validation set selects the model. A budget therefore counts all soundscape labels the training uses.
  • Every point fine-tunes the whole network. A label-efficient method (frozen backbone, or reweighting only) may need fewer labels; that has not been tested.

Notation used on this page

FocalA recording aimed at one bird (here from Xeno-Canto). The source domain. Shown in orange.
SoundscapeA recording from a fixed field recorder (passive acoustic monitoring, “PAM”). The target domain. Shown in blue.
ClipA 5-second piece of audio: the unit the model scores.
Fold, cross-validation (CV)The development sites are cut into 3 groups. Each fold scores one group; training uses the other two.
Held-out sitesSites whose audio and labels a model never learned from; it is scored on them.
NA number of labeled soundscape clips.
0 labeled clipsThe pretrained model, unchanged.
“All” / all labeled clipsThe pretrained model fine-tuned on every labeled soundscape clip of the training sites, never on held-out or test sites, with no focal training data. It defines γ = 100%.
Scores and “points”AP, AUC, cmAP and activations are shown out of 100. A change in points is the difference of two such numbers: 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points (not 6.7% more correct clips).
APAverage precision: how near the top of the model's ranking the clips with a species are; 100 = all first (worked example).
Soundscape AP (primary species)The main metric: AP on held-out soundscape clips, averaged over primary species.
AUC, ROC-AUCHow often a clip with the species scores above a clip without it. 100 = always, 50 = chance.
Primary speciesSecondary species with at least 30 positive clips in the soundscape sites being scored.
GapAP of the model trained on all labeled training-site soundscapes minus AP of the pretrained model.
95% interval, [a, b]The range the number covers when recording sites (focal: recordists) are resampled 1,000 times. A change whose range includes zero cannot be told apart from no change; this is not proof of no effect.
Reliably better / worseThe 95% interval of the change lies entirely above / below zero.
Secondary speciesSpecies with at least 5 positive clips in both the focal and soundscape sets being scored.
γ, share of the gap closedA method's gain divided by the gap. 0% = no better than the pretrained model, 100% = equal to the all-labels model, below 0% = worse, above 100% = better than it.
How γ is averaged hereγ is computed in each fold and then averaged (“Mean γ”). The results pages divide mean gain by mean gap instead, so the two differ slightly (59% here and 60% there for 250 labels). “γ, same 5” and “γ, same 7” use mean gain over mean gap on a fixed species set.
2/3, “2 folds”On how many of the 3 folds something holds (or a species was scored).
Failing prototypeA prototype whose ΔAUC is below −10 points.
Δ, ΔAUC (prototypes)A prototype's AUC on soundscape clips minus its AUC on focal clips. Negative means it detects its bird worse in the field. Not a comparison between methods.
Empty clipA soundscape clip labeled with none of the 21 species.
Positive / negative clipA clip that does / does not contain the species in question.
– or —Not run, not scored or not applicable.
