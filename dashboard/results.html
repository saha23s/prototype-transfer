What this page shows

  • No method without soundscape labels shows a meaningful gain. The best mean change is +0.003 on the scored species. When every fold scores the same species it is +0.004 (5 species) or +0.001 (7 species).
  • How firmly this is ruled out varies. For both training-free methods and both no-projection variants, the 95% interval of the gap closed ends below 30% on every fold. Other fine-tuned models have wider intervals: in 7 of all 36 method–fold pairs, closing half the gap is still inside the interval.
  • Among the single ingredients, only one effect is clear on all three species sets: adding pseudo-labels lowers AP (scored species: −0.086, 95% interval −0.122 to −0.061; same 7: −0.070, 95% interval −0.111 to −0.029).
  • Real background with species overlap is not a dependable gain. Against plain focal fine-tuning it is +0.020 on the scored species (interval includes zero) and +0.020 on the 5 shared species (interval above zero), but ±0.000 on the 7 species eligible on every fold. The effect of freezing the backbone is not established: its interval includes zero on all three sets.
  • With labels, prototypes detect better on soundscapes (mean AUC 0.840 → 0.920; with matched negatives 0.775 → 0.890; focal barely moves). But better prototypes have also occurred without better AP: a frozen backbone (0.840 → 0.865) and, with matched negatives, plain focal fine-tuning (0.775 → 0.826) do not raise class AP.
  • Read failing-prototype counts with care. They depend on which soundscape clips serve as negatives. With real background mixing the failing share rises from 42% to 55% under the protocol, but with negatives matched to focal it is 57%, against 57% for the pretrained model.

All fine-tuning starts from the pretrained model: 2 warm-up epochs (prototypes and last layer), 12 epochs of the whole network (skipped when frozen), prototype projection onto training patches, then 4 last-layer epochs. The last epoch is used. Mixing is applied with probability 0.5 (background at −5 to 20 dB SNR; the overlaid species 0 to 10 dB below the original).

The main 7 species and the other 5

The test split scores 12 species: the main 7 (White-crowned Sparrow, American Pipit, Rock Wren, Dark-eyed Junco, Clark's Nutcracker, Hermit Thrush, American Robin) and 5 rarer ones (Fox Sparrow, Mountain Bluebird, Cassin's Finch, Yellow-rumped Warbler, Mountain Chickadee). Means over 3 folds, each fold averaging the same species. Do not rank methods on the other 5: a species has only 1 to 34 positive clips per held-out fold, often from one site, so one clip can move its AP a lot. For the model trained on all labels the fold changes are −0.059, +0.054, −0.041, although its AUC on these species rises from 0.871 to 0.942. No intervals are given for this group because resampling sites often leaves a species with no positive clip.

Status and limitations

  • These are cross-validation results. On the test split only the pretrained model has been scored so far (soundscape AP 0.644, 95% interval 0.498 to 0.722); the test sites are kept for final comparisons.
  • One training seed per method. Differences of a few hundredths between trained methods are within what seeds or folds could produce.
  • The frozen-backbone ablations were added after the other runs had been launched. Treat them as exploratory.
  • Comparisons on this page are not corrected for the number of methods and folds.
  • The two fixed species sets and the intervals between adapted models were added after the first results were seen. The protocol's headline is still the change on each fold's primary species.
  • All fine-tuned focal models use one fixed schedule and mixing strength; no hyperparameters were tuned for them.
  • The training-free baselines are simple ones. Their result does not rule out better label-free adaptation.
  • With 3 folds of about 23 sites, a missing “reliably better” does not show that a method has no effect.
