Prototype transfer: focal → soundscape bird recognition

Initial results

Can unlabeled soundscape audio close the focal-to-soundscape gap of a prototype-based bird classifier?

1 Experimental setup

Task. Multi-label recognition of 21 High Sierra Nevada bird species. The source model is AudioProtoPNet, pretrained on Xeno-Canto focal recordings; a species score is a non-negative weighted sum of the activations of its five prototypes (problem formulation).

Data. Focal clips are split by recordist into 3,695 / 608 / 1,157 train / validation / test clips. Soundscapes are 5 s passive-recording clips, split by site: 70 development sites and 30 test sites (3,600 clips). Development uses 3-fold site-grouped cross-validation (CV), holding out 24 / 23 / 23 sites. Adaptation is inductive: a method may use unlabeled audio of training sites, is then frozen, and is scored on sites it has not heard.

Metrics. The headline metric is soundscape macro AP over primary species, those with at least 30 positive clips in the scored split (6, 7, 6 species on the three folds; 7 on test). We also report ROC-AUC, cmAP over secondary species (at least 5 positives in both domains), focal ROC-AUC, and the number of prototypes that fail to transfer. Intervals are paired 95% cluster bootstraps (soundscape clips resampled by site, focal clips by recordist; 1,000 resamples).

Test evaluations. The test split was scored for the source model, the five source-free adaptation methods, full focal fine-tuning and the model trained on all labels. The settings of the five adaptation methods were fixed before any of their runs; each adapted on the 7,200 clips of the training sites.

Baselines. Source-free adaptation follows the audio code and settings of Boudiaf et al. (2023): Tent, pseudo-labelling, Dropout Student, NOTELA and SHOT, each species treated as a binary problem. Because this backbone has no batch normalisation, the layer-normalisation weights are adapted; prototypes and class weights stay fixed. We also compare two training-free methods, focal fine-tuning with and without unlabeled soundscape audio, and, as references, training on labeled soundscape clips (label curve). Details are in Table A1.

Cross-validation. Dropout Student has the highest mean AP among methods without soundscape labels, +0.067 over the source model (γ = 45%), and is reliably better on 2 of 3 folds. The gain persists when every fold scores the same 5 or 7 species (+0.068 and +0.059). It still misses our target, closing half the gap on 1 of 3 folds. Pseudo-labelling (+0.016) and SHOT (+0.011) are reliably better on all folds, by small margins; Tent is not better (−0.006). None of the 12 training-free and fine-tuning methods is reliably better on any fold. For both training-free methods and both frozen, no-projection variants, the interval of the share of the gap closed ends below 30% on every fold.

Held-out test sites. The cross-validation advantage is not confirmed on test. None of the five source-free methods differs reliably from the source model. Dropout Student keeps the largest point estimate, but its gain is +0.027 [−0.074, +0.109], against +0.067 in cross-validation. Its share of the gap, 15%, is poorly determined (95% interval −109% to 73%). The split can still separate some differences: full focal fine-tuning is reliably worse (−0.040) and training on all labels reliably better (+0.178).

Labels as a reference. With 250 labeled soundscape clips the mean CV gain is +0.089, above every method without labels; with all labeled clips it is +0.150.

NOTELA versus Dropout Student. NOTELA adds neighbour smoothing to Dropout Student's targets. Here it averages +0.021 with no fold reliably better, and +0.002 on the same 7 species, whereas its authors report it ahead of Dropout Student on all six of their test sets by sample-wise mAP, with a different model. In the binary form used for multi-label data, the smoothing term lowers a species' target unless similar clips score it above 0.5 on average. We have not tested whether this explains the difference.

Prototype detection. Training on labels raises mean soundscape prototype AUC from 0.840 to 0.920 (matched negatives: 0.775 to 0.890) while focal AUC barely moves. Dropout Student raises it without labels, to 0.875 (matched: 0.821), with prototype vectors and class weights unchanged; on test its failing prototypes fall from 26 to 15.

Better prototypes are not sufficient. A frozen-backbone fine-tune raises soundscape prototype AUC (0.840 to 0.865), as does full focal fine-tuning with matched negatives (0.775 to 0.826); neither improves class AP. These comparisons are observational and do not show that prototype quality causes AP.

The failure count depends on the negatives. With real background mixing, the failing share rises from 42% to 55% under the protocol, but with matched negatives it is 57%, against 57% for the source model.

Focal retention. On test, NOTELA is the only source-free method that loses more than 0.01 of focal AUC.

Pseudo-labels hurt. The only effect that is clear on all three species sets is negative: adding the source model's soft predictions as a training target lowers AP (−0.086 [−0.122, −0.061] on the scored species; −0.070 [−0.111, −0.029] on the same 7). This is a training loss during focal fine-tuning, distinct from the source-free pseudo-labelling baseline.

Mixing and freezing are inconclusive. Real background with species overlap, against plain focal fine-tuning, is +0.020 on the scored species (interval includes zero), +0.020 on the 5 shared species (interval above zero) and ±0.000 on the 7 fixed species. The interval for freezing the backbone includes zero on all three sets.

6 Limitations

  • One training seed per method. Seed-to-seed variation has not been measured for any method. It may matter for Dropout Student and NOTELA, which rely on random noise during adaptation.
  • Few sites. Each fold holds out about 23 sites and test has 30, so intervals are wide. A missing reliable difference does not show that a method has no effect.
  • No correction for multiple comparisons across methods, folds or species.
  • Inherited hyperparameters. The source-free methods use their authors' settings without tuning here. The authors selected hyperparameters with labels on a High Sierras soundscape set, with a different model; we have not checked whether its recordings overlap ours. The results are not the best each method could reach.
  • Adapted implementations. Layer-normalisation weights replace batch-normalisation weights. Student noise is random skipping of network blocks (probability 0.2); the authors' additional dropout on a pooled feature has no counterpart here. Test-time methods that adapt on the scored audio are outside this protocol, and the training-free baselines are simple ones.
  • The test split has now been seen for these methods. A method changed after this can no longer claim a clean held-out score.
  • Post-hoc analyses. The frozen-backbone ablations, the fixed species sets and the intervals between adapted models were added after first results were seen. The protocol's headline remains the change on each fold's primary species.
  • Focal scores are in-domain for the source model, which was pretrained on Xeno-Canto.
