Initial results

Can unlabeled soundscape audio close the focal-to-soundscape gap of a prototype-based bird classifier?

Without soundscape labels, Dropout Student looked promising in development, but its gain was not confirmed at the test sites. None of the five adaptation methods tested there showed a reliable gain.

New to these numbers? See the metrics and how to read them, and which data is used for training and scoring.

Detailed results

Everything behind the summary: setup, every method and metric, per-species results, limitations and appendix.

1 Experimental setup

Task. Multi-label recognition of 21 High Sierra Nevada bird species. The source model is AudioProtoPNet, pretrained on Xeno-Canto focal recordings; a species score is a non-negative weighted sum of the activations of its five prototypes (problem formulation).

Data. Focal clips are split by recordist into 3,695 / 608 / 1,157 train / validation / test clips. Soundscapes are 5 s passive-recording clips, split by site: 70 development sites and 30 test sites (3,600 clips). Development uses 3-fold site-grouped cross-validation (CV), holding out 24 / 23 / 23 sites. Adaptation is inductive: a method may use unlabeled audio of training sites, is then frozen, and is scored on sites it has not heard.

Metrics. All scores are shown out of 100 and changes in points. The headline metric is soundscape macro AP over primary species, those with at least 30 positive clips in the scored split (6, 7, 6 species on the three folds; 7 on test). We also report ROC-AUC, cmAP over secondary species (at least 5 positives in both domains), focal ROC-AUC, and the number of prototypes that fail to transfer. Intervals are paired 95% cluster bootstraps (soundscape clips resampled by site, focal clips by recordist; 1,000 resamples).

Test evaluations. The test split was scored for the source model, the five source-free adaptation methods, full focal fine-tuning and the model trained on all labels. The settings of the five adaptation methods were fixed before any of their runs; each adapted on the 7,200 clips of the training sites.

Baselines. Source-free adaptation follows the audio code and settings of Boudiaf et al. (2023): Tent, pseudo-labelling, Dropout Student, NOTELA and SHOT, each species treated as a binary problem. Because this backbone has no batch normalisation, the layer-normalisation weights are adapted; prototypes and class weights stay fixed. We also compare two training-free methods, focal fine-tuning with and without unlabeled soundscape audio, and, as references, training on labeled soundscape clips (label curve). Details are in Table A1.

Cross-validation. Dropout Student has the highest mean AP among methods without soundscape labels, +6.7 points over the source model (γ = 45%), and is reliably better on 2 of 3 folds. The gain persists when every fold scores the same 5 or 7 species (+6.8 points and +5.9 points). It still misses our target, closing half the gap on 1 of 3 folds. Pseudo-labelling (+1.6 points) and SHOT (+1.1 points) are reliably better on all folds, by small margins; Tent is not better (−0.6 points). None of the 12 training-free and fine-tuning methods is reliably better on any fold. For both training-free methods and both frozen, no-projection variants, the interval of the share of the gap closed ends below 30% on every fold.

Held-out test sites. The cross-validation advantage is not confirmed on test. None of the five source-free methods differs reliably from the source model. Dropout Student keeps the largest point estimate, but its gain is +2.7 points [−7.4, +10.9], against +6.7 points in cross-validation. Its share of the gap, 15%, is poorly determined (95% interval −109% to 73%). The split can still separate some differences: full focal fine-tuning is reliably worse (−4.0 points) and training on all labels reliably better (+17.8 points).

Labels as a reference. With 250 labeled soundscape clips the mean CV gain is +8.9 points, above every method without labels; with all labeled clips it is +15.0 points.

NOTELA versus Dropout Student. NOTELA adds neighbour smoothing to Dropout Student's targets. Here it averages +2.1 points with no fold reliably better, and +0.2 points on the same 7 species, whereas its authors report it ahead of Dropout Student on all six of their test sets by sample-wise mAP, with a different model. In the binary form used for multi-label data, the smoothing term lowers a species' target unless similar clips score it above 50% on average. We have not tested whether this explains the difference.

Prototype detection. Training on labels raises mean soundscape prototype AUC from 84.0 to 92.0 (matched negatives: 77.5 to 89.0) while focal AUC barely moves. Dropout Student raises it without labels, to 87.5 (matched: 82.1), with prototype vectors and class weights unchanged; on test its failing prototypes fall from 26 to 15.

Better prototypes are not sufficient. A frozen-backbone fine-tune raises soundscape prototype AUC (84.0 to 86.5), as does full focal fine-tuning with matched negatives (77.5 to 82.6); neither improves class AP. These comparisons are observational and do not show that prototype quality causes AP.

The failure count depends on the negatives. With real background mixing, the failing share rises from 42% to 55% under the protocol, but with matched negatives it is 57%, against 57% for the source model.

Focal retention. On test, NOTELA is the only source-free method that loses more than 1 point of focal AUC.

The gain is concentrated. Pseudo-labels hurt. The only effect that is clear on all three species sets is negative: adding the source model's soft predictions as a training target lowers AP (−8.6 points [−12.2, −6.1] on the scored species; −7.0 points [−11.1, −2.9] on the same 7). This is a training loss during focal fine-tuning, distinct from the source-free pseudo-labelling baseline.

Mixing and freezing are inconclusive. Real background with species overlap, against plain focal fine-tuning, is +2.0 points on the scored species (interval includes zero), +2.0 points on the 5 shared species (interval above zero) and ±0.0 points on the 7 fixed species. The interval for freezing the backbone includes zero on all three sets.

6 Limitations

  • One training seed per method. Seed-to-seed variation has not been measured for any method. It may matter for Dropout Student and NOTELA, which rely on random noise during adaptation.
  • Few sites. Each fold holds out about 23 sites and test has 30, so intervals are wide. A missing reliable difference does not show that a method has no effect.
  • No correction for multiple comparisons across methods, folds or species.
  • Inherited hyperparameters. The source-free methods use their authors' settings without tuning here. The authors selected hyperparameters with labels on a High Sierras soundscape set, with a different model; we have not checked whether its recordings overlap ours. The results are not the best each method could reach.
  • Adapted implementations. Layer-normalisation weights replace batch-normalisation weights. Student noise is random skipping of network blocks (probability 20%); the authors' additional dropout on a pooled feature has no counterpart here. Test-time methods that adapt on the scored audio are outside this protocol, and the training-free baselines are simple ones.
  • The test split has now been seen for these methods. A method changed after this can no longer claim a clean held-out score.
  • Post-hoc analyses. The frozen-backbone ablations, the fixed species sets and the intervals between adapted models were added after first results were seen. The protocol's headline remains the change on each fold's primary species.
  • Focal scores are in-domain for the source model, which was pretrained on Xeno-Canto.

Notation used on this page

FocalA recording aimed at one bird (here from Xeno-Canto). The source domain. Shown in orange.
SoundscapeA recording from a fixed field recorder (passive acoustic monitoring, “PAM”). The target domain. Shown in blue.
ClipA 5-second piece of audio: the unit the model scores.
Development / test sitesDevelopment: the 70 non-test sites, used to compare methods. Test: 30 sites kept for final numbers.
Fold, cross-validation (CV)The development sites are cut into 3 groups. Each fold scores one group; training uses the other two.
Held-out sitesSites whose audio and labels a model never learned from; it is scored on them.
“All” / all labeled clipsThe pretrained model fine-tuned on every labeled soundscape clip of the training sites, never on held-out or test sites, with no focal training data. It defines γ = 100%.
NA number of labeled soundscape clips.
Scores and “points”AP, AUC, cmAP and activations are shown out of 100. A change in points is the difference of two such numbers: 71.0 to 77.8 is +6.7 points (not 6.7% more correct clips).
APAverage precision: how near the top of the model's ranking the clips with a species are; 100 = all first (worked example).
Soundscape AP (primary species)The main metric: AP on held-out soundscape clips, averaged over primary species.
AUC, ROC-AUCHow often a clip with the species scores above a clip without it. 100 = always, 50 = chance.
Primary speciesSecondary species with at least 30 positive clips in the soundscape sites being scored.
GapAP of the model trained on all labeled training-site soundscapes minus AP of the pretrained model.
95% interval, [a, b]The range the number covers when recording sites (focal: recordists) are resampled 1,000 times. A change whose range includes zero cannot be told apart from no change; this is not proof of no effect.
Reliably better / worseThe 95% interval of the change lies entirely above / below zero.
Secondary speciesSpecies with at least 5 positive clips in both the focal and soundscape sets being scored.
cmAPClass-mean average precision: AP averaged over species; here over the secondary species.
Sample-wise mAPAverage precision computed per clip over species, then averaged over clips. Not the metric of this study; quoted from other authors.
ΔAP, “change”A method's AP minus the pretrained (source) model's on the same clips. Positive is better.
Δ, ΔAUC (prototypes)A prototype's AUC on soundscape clips minus its AUC on focal clips. Negative means it detects its bird worse in the field. Not a comparison between methods.
Failing prototypeA prototype whose ΔAUC is below −10 points.
Matched negativesOnly soundscape clips containing another target bird count as negatives, as for focal clips. “sc” stands for soundscape and “sc matched” for this version.
K, PkEach species has K = 5 prototypes (learned sound patterns); P1 to P5 name them, e.g. “whcspa P3”.
γ, share of the gap closedA method's gain divided by the gap. 0% = no better than the pretrained model, 100% = equal to the all-labels model, below 0% = worse, above 100% = better than it.
How γ is averaged hereMean gain over the folds divided by the mean gap over the folds. The label curve page computes γ in each fold and then averages, so it can differ slightly (59% there, 60% here, for 250 labels).
↑ / ↓ (folds)Number of folds on which the method is reliably better / worse.
↓ in a column titleIn the secondary-metrics table, ↓ after a column name means lower is better.
± valueStandard deviation over the 3 folds: how much they differ. Not a confidence interval.
Bold, underlineBest and second-best printed value among the methods compared. Says nothing about reliability.
*The test interval of the change excludes zero.
2/3, “2 folds”On how many of the 3 folds something holds (or a species was scored).
Criteria met: gap · AUC · protos · focalOn how many of 3 folds each success criterion is met: gap closed, AUC not below, failing prototypes halved, focal retained.
– or —Not run, not scored or not applicable.
Positive / negative clipA clip that does / does not contain the species in question.
SNR, dBSignal-to-noise ratio in decibels: how loud the bird is relative to the added background.
